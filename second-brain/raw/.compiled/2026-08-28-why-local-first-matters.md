# Why Local-First Software Matters

The cloud-first era taught us to store everything on someone else's server. Local-first flips that: your data lives on your machine first, and syncs optionally.

## The Problem With Cloud-First

- **Vendor lock-in** — Switching services means migrating years of data
- **Latency** — Every save, every read, every edit goes through a server
- **Privacy** — Your notes, documents, and thoughts live on infrastructure you don't control
- **Availability** — No internet? No access to your own files

## What Local-First Actually Means

- Files are plain text on disk (markdown, CSV, JSON)
- No proprietary database format
- You own the storage layer
- Sync is optional, not required
- Offline-first by default

## The Obsidian Model

Obsidian popularized this approach for note-taking. A vault is just a folder of `.md` files. No lock-in, nothing proprietary. You can open your notes in any text editor, grep them, version-control them with git.

## Key Takeaways

- Local-first means your data lives on your machine, not someone else's server
- Plain text files (markdown) are the most durable storage format
- Vendor lock-in is the biggest risk of cloud-first tools
- Offline access and privacy are natural side effects, not features you have to request
- The tradeoff: you're responsible for your own backups
